MAGMA/MISTA Multilevel CT Reconstruction — Status & Root-Cause Findings
Parth Brahmbhatt | Prepared for Dr. Zichao (Wendy) Di
================================================================================

1. GOAL
--------
Accelerate ell1/TV-regularized CT reconstruction

    min_x  ||A x - b||^2 + lambda * R(x)

using multilevel/multigrid optimization, by analogy with:
  - Nash (2000) MG/OPT, and Wendy's own multigrid-for-ptychography paper
    (Fung & Di, SIAM J. Imaging Sci. 2020, doi:10.1137/18M1223915), and
  - Panos Parpas's multilevel composite-optimization papers:
      * MAGMA: Hovhannisyan, Parpas, Zafeiriou, SIAM J. Imaging Sci. 2016
        ("Multilevel Accelerated Gradient Mirror Descent Algorithm")
      * MISTA: Parpas, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 2017
        ("A Multilevel Proximal Gradient Algorithm for a Class of
        Composite Optimization Problems")

2. RESULT
---------
Not achieved. The code runs and produces reconstructions, but the
multilevel/coarse-correction step does not deliver the accuracy-per-unit-work
improvement over plain FISTA that the papers' theory promises, across every
variant that was tried (see Section 4). This is not attributed to an
implementation bug that a fix would resolve; it is a structural mismatch
between what MAGMA/MISTA's multilevel construction assumes and what a
tomography forward operator actually looks like. Section 5 explains why, and
Section 6 lists concrete directions that would need to be pursued to make
multilevel acceleration work for this problem.

3. WHY PLAIN MG/OPT WAS RULED OUT FIRST
----------------------------------------
Standard MG/OPT (Nash 2000), the framework behind Wendy's MG_tomo starter
code, is derived for smooth objectives. Our objective has a non-smooth
ell1/TV term, so a direct MG/OPT coarse-grid correction is not well defined
for it (this was flagged already in the 7/21/25 SASSy presentation,
slide "Multi Grid Approach to Tomography Reconstruction": "MG-opt for the
composite objective will not work"). That is what motivated moving to
Parpas's MAGMA/MISTA, which were built specifically to extend multilevel
acceleration to the smooth + non-smooth composite case.

             (first-order coherence identity holds to ~1e-15, objective

             the best entry point for anyone picking the diagnosis back
             up -- start at tests/test_05_benchmark.m.

5. ROOT CAUSE
-------------
MAGMA/MISTA's multilevel construction, as published, assumes a
dictionary-style coarsening where the fine and coarse problems keep the
same rank character. The tomography forward operator does not have a
natural analogue of that under image-only coarsening, and every attempt to
patch the coherence/smoothing term to compensate ran into the
ill-conditioning of the CT operator. In more detail:

  a) Different notion of "coarse level."
     In the MAGMA paper (Sec 3.1.1, p.1838) and in Panos's own
     SolveMRFISTA.m, the restriction operator R coarsens *columns of the
     dictionary matrix A* in a LASSO/face-recognition problem -- A there is
     a fixed, reasonably well-behaved dictionary. Applied to tomography,
     "coarsening" was implemented as downsampling the image grid and
     rebuilding L_coarse = L_fine * P algebraically, while the
     measurement/sinogram side (rows of L, i.e. angles/detector bins) was
     initially left at full fine resolution ("one-sided" restriction).

  b) That one-sided restriction inverts the problem's structure.
     The fine CT system is underdetermined (few projection angles, many
     unknown pixels). A one-sided coarse system A_c = A*P keeps the same
     number of measurements but a quarter of the unknowns, so it becomes
     comparatively OVERdetermined -- the opposite character from the fine
     problem. tomo_MISTA/tests/test_05_benchmark.m measures this directly:
     with one-sided restriction the required first-order-coherence
     correction term v_H (Eq. 3.3 in the MAGMA paper) becomes very large,
     so the coarse-corrected search direction collapses to "little more
     than a gradient step" -- no real acceleration, and a source of
     instability at high lambda / low iteration counts. A "two-sided" fix
     (also restricting the sinogram, so A_c = R_s * A * P) shrinks v_H
     substantially and is the theoretically correct move, but was only
     validated on internal consistency checks, not shown to beat FISTA in
     practice.

  c) Panos's own code flags the same approximation as shaky.
     SolveMRFISTA.m (Panos Code/Archive/) contains this comment verbatim:
       "WORNING: this is only heuristic! With smoothed coarse the
       coherence becomes: grad(F_H)=grad(f_h). Not quite what we want."
     i.e., even in the original author's code, the smoothed-coherence
     shortcut used to make the coarse model differentiable is flagged as
     not rigorous -- before it was ever applied to a harder, more
     ill-conditioned operator.

  d) The CT operator amplifies these approximation errors.
     The tomography system matrix is severely ill-conditioned (see the
     SASSy slide: "Projection Matrix (Very ill-conditioned)"), far more so
     than the dictionaries MAGMA was validated on. Any residual error in
     v_H, or bias from the fixed-mu pseudo-Huber smoothing of the L1 term
     used to make the composite gradient differentiable (mu was held
     constant rather than annealed, unlike the smoothing schedule implied
     by the paper's Sec. 2.5 framework), gets amplified rather than
     damped by L^T*L.

  e) Non-negativity was patched in outside the theory.
     The physical image must be >= 0, but MAGMA's convergence proof (Sec.
     3, Lemma 2.1/2.3/2.5, Thm 2.6) is stated for the declared
     g(x)=lambda*||x||_1 only. Clipping to zero after the fact
     (max(0, y_{k+1}), visible commented out in later solver versions) is
     mathematically equivalent to the correct proximal operator ONLY if
     applied identically at every level (fine and coarse); it was not
     applied consistently everywhere the theory requires it, which can
     produce a coarse-corrected direction that argues for pixel values the
     fine-level constraint would reject.

6. RECOMMENDED DIRECTIONS (if this is picked back up)
-------------------------------------------------------
These are not "fixes" to the existing code so much as different starting
assumptions:

  1. Rebuild the coarse forward operator from the physics, not algebra.
     Instead of L_coarse = R * L_fine * P (a purely algebraic/Galerkin
     restriction of the fine matrix), regenerate the coarse-level Radon
     system directly at the coarse image resolution AND a coarser
     angle/detector sampling using the same generator (XTM_Tensor_XH), the
     way classical geometric multigrid re-discretizes a PDE operator on
     each grid rather than algebraically projecting the fine one. This is
     the tomography analogue of the "tailored restriction and
     prolongation operators that exploit the hierarchical structure" Wendy
     used for ptychography (Fung & Di 2020) -- domain-specific R/P built
     from the imaging physics, not generic operators borrowed from a
     dictionary-learning problem.

  2. Make restriction two-sided everywhere, consistently.
     Carry tomo_MISTA's Piece-5 two-sided construction (restrict both
     image and sinogram) through as the only supported mode, and drop the
     one-sided path entirely -- it is diagnosably worse by the algorithm's
     own theory (large v_H), not just empirically.

  3. Anneal mu instead of fixing it.
     Tie the L1-smoothing parameter mu to the current desired accuracy
     (shrink it as the algorithm proceeds, or per level) per the
     smoothing framework in MAGMA Sec. 2.5, rather than using one fixed
     mu = 1e-4 for the whole run. This removes a persistent bias in v_H
     that has nothing to do with the multilevel idea itself.

  4. Apply the non-negativity constraint identically at every level.
     Fold x>=0 explicitly into g(x) at both fine and coarse levels
     (g(x) = lambda*||x||_1 + indicator{x>=0}, whose prox is still
     closed-form: max(x - lambda/L, 0)) instead of an ad hoc clip applied
     inconsistently, so the coarse subproblem and the fine subproblem are
     solving the same constrained problem.

  5. Benchmark against a work-matched baseline across regimes, not just
     unit tests. tomo_MISTA's Part A/B benchmark suggests multilevel
     correction may only pay off in the severely rank-deficient /
     limited-angle regime (few projection angles), not for well-determined
     scans. Before investing further in the MAGMA/MISTA formulation
     specifically, it would be worth mapping out whether *any* fixed
     variant beats FISTA at equal computational work, and in which regime
     -- using the equivalent-work accounting already implemented in
     magma_solver.m / mrfista_solver.m.

  6. Consider a geometric-multigrid (FAS-style) reformulation instead of
     forcing MAGMA's decision rule.
     Rather than the current heuristic "attempt a coarse step vs. take a
     gradient step" branch (kappa/theta/Kd thresholds), a Full
     Approximation Scheme V-cycle -- coarse level solves for a defect
     correction against a properly re-discretized coarse operator (per
     item 1), with an ISTA/FISTA proximal smoother applied pre- and
     post-correction -- is closer to what the pre_post_smoothing /
     pre_post_laplacian experiments were reaching for ad hoc, but would
     need to be derived and justified formally rather than patched in.
